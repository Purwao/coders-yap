# Why Portfolio?
A strong portfolio is one of many key factors for developers and designers that can catch the attention of a potential client or employer. Therefore, I made my [second website portfolio](https://purwao.github.io/porto-2/). The [first attempt](https://purwao.vercel.app) made me realize that it felt rather like a desktop experience. So, on this second time, I'm going to focus on the website feeling, focus on clarity, maintaining my personal branding, while learning a design I stumbled upon along the way, which I believe is called brutalism.

# How to Create
To create a strong portfolio, you must first know who your audience is. In my case, I consider my audience as recruiters, developers, or just someone curious about what I build. Also, try to be mindful of how people experience this site. Most people prefer things that are simple and readable to them, where projects speak for themselves, and explanations are clear without being overwhelming.
 
Second, you need to think about the project you will share on the website. People tend to care about the finished version; they never ask about what happened behind the curtain. The project I've shared in this portfolio is but a grain of sand on the beach. Small but meaningful. Some are experimental, while others are to solve my everyday problems. Whether it is a guestbook I made, an Android finance tracker, a blog, or a note app, these serve as a purpose for me to become the kind of developer I strive to be. For me, each one of them represents a problem, a solution, an approach, and lesson .

Third, a good name for website should be memorable, relevant, and straightforward, making it easier to access for users visiting your site. Using your name is one of many ways to help create a clear personal brand intended for recruiters or employers. Keep in mind that simplicity is also the key to a more professional and clean way, so that the visitors don't get confused. It also helps them to create a positive impression and reflects the seriousness of your work. 


Lastly, choosing a hosting platform matters. It is also important because it affects the accessibility and reliability of a site. Platform like Squarespace and Wix offer its users with a drag-and-drop web builder, which makes them suitable and friendly for beginners but it sacrifices creativity because of limited customization. On the other hand, WordPress offers the flexibility you need and more features, but it requires more effort and maintenance.

For developers, I suggest using GitHub Pages, Vercel, and Netlify for popular free options. These platforms allow users to host static websites at no cost and provide free subdomains, making them ideal for portfolio projects.

# Design and Technical
Design plays a key part in how visitors experience a site. For this website, I chose a brutalism-inspired design, which emphasizes bold typography, sharp edges, and minimal decoration. This style may feel raw or unconventional, but I find it reflects my personality and makes the site stand out. At the same time, I made sure the layout remains clear and user-friendly, so visitors can easily navigate between projects, skills, and contact information.

Mobile responsiveness is also important, since many visitors use phones or tablets. Even with the bold, striking elements of brutalism, the site adapts to different screen sizes, keeping all content readable and accessible.

Fast loading times were another focus. A study found that even a one‑second delay in page load time can reduce conversions by about 7%. By minimizing unnecessary animations and optimizing images, the portfolio remains lightweight and quick, ensuring visitors can explore projects without delays.

# Structuring a Portfolio
A well-structured portfolio should include several key pages to guide the visitor through your work. 
Here I list what I believe are the key aspects:
- Homepage – Provides a quick overview of who you are, your skills, and what visitors can expect from your site. It’s often the first impression, so clarity and simplicity are important.
- About Me – Shares your personal bio, background, and motivations. This helps visitors understand your journey, values, and personality.
- Portfolio / Projects – The main showcase of your work, including images, descriptions, and links to live projects or code. This page demonstrates your skills and problem-solving abilities.
- Blog (optional) – A blog is a great way to show your writing about projects, tutorials, or reflections. A blog can show thought leadership, communication skills, and learning.
- Contact Page – Provides clear ways for visitors, recruiters, or potential clients to reach you, such as email, social links, or a contact form.

# What Project To Put
When presenting your work, I believe it is important to be reflective. It doesn't have to be polished; include your projects that showcase your skills, creativity, and growth, even if they're experimental. For each project, consider :
- The challenge – What problem or idea did you explore?
- Your approach – How did you experiment or implement a solution? What technologies or techniques did you use?
- What you learned – Even if the project isn’t perfect, what skills, insights, or lessons did it give you?

This approach lets visitors see not only what you can build, but also how you think, experiment, and grow as a developer. A well-curated portfolio balances polished projects with meaningful experiments, showing both competence and creativity.

It’s equally important to keep your portfolio updated by regularly adding new projects or improvements, so returning visitors can see your growth and progress. Tools like Google Analytics and user feedback can provide valuable insights into how people use your site and what could be improved.

Ultimately, a well-crafted and maintained portfolio not only highlights your abilities and creativity but also encourages visitors, clients, or recruiters to take action, whether that’s reaching out, collaborating, or learning from your work.
