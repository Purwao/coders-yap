# The Story That Started It All

It’s not like I just woke up one day and went, *“Y’know what? I think building a blog would be nice…”*

Nope—this all started in the middle of the night, when my not-so-quiet brain suddenly decided,  
*“Oh boy, I’m bored as heck.”*

And you know how it goes—while normal people would probably call it a night and get some sleep, I?  
I spun up a brand-new project.  
Yep, right there, in the middle of the night.  
Meanwhile, my other two projects sat in the corner of my screen, throwing death stares at me like, *“Bro… seriously?”*

---

# Why Build a Blog?

Surprisingly, I had a decent reason this time.  
Why build a blog, and not something else?

At the time, I had two things bugging me:

- I needed something to help me track where I went wrong—so I wouldn't fall into the same traps twice.
- I wanted something cool I could flex to my friends.

So… isn’t a blog the perfect answer to both?  
(Okay, mostly the first one.)

Someone once told me:

> “Let it be, and it’ll last for 10 seconds.  
> Document it, and it’ll last for an aeon.”  
>
> — My friend, probably

# What Did I Use?

I mainly used **Next.js** and **Tailwind CSS**, plus a few other helpful libraries that made things smoother.

If you're new to this kind of thing, don’t worry—I'll break it down so you get the rough idea.

---

## Next.js

A React-based framework that’s great for building fast, SEO-friendly web apps.  
Perfect for a blog like this, with built-in support for static site generation, dynamic routes, and more.

---

## Tailwind CSS

A utility-first CSS framework that makes styling like a walk in the park.  
You barely need to write any custom CSS—just combine the right utility classes and you're good to go.

---

## Honorable Mentions

### Lenis Scroll

This hidden gem makes the scroll experience buttery smooth.  
Try comparing it with other websites if you'd like and you’ll notice the BIG difference!  
Big shoutout to **@Firz** for introducing me to this one :D

### Giscus

This is the engine behind the comment section!  
Just install the Giscus GitHub app on your repo, mess with your code a little, and bam you acquire a working comment system.

### MDX

The secret sauce that lets me mix Markdown and JSX.  
That’s how I get to write blog posts while still sneaking in React components when needed.
